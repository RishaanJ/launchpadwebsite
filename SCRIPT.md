# Launchpad Launch Video — Script Comparison (v2)

Both scripts were rewritten after the first drafts came out sounding like AI keynote copy — stacked fragments, dramatic name reveals, "Not X. Not Y." patterns. These versions are written the way the Clicky video actually sounds: one person talking to a camera in full, natural sentences. Format assumption for both: talking head, no b-roll required.

- **VERSION_A.txt** — "The question" (starts with the viewer)
- **VERSION_B.txt** — "The straight talk" (starts with the announcement)

---

## Version A — "The question"

### Creative direction
Opens by asking the viewer something personal: "Have you ever built something you were actually proud of? ... Where is it now?" The whole first minute is about *their* experience, and Launchpad only shows up once the viewer already feels the gap. The name lands on "So we're going to." — the pivot from problem to plan.

### Tone and pacing
Warm, a little wry ("a GitHub repo with one star. Yours."). Builds slowly, spends its energy on making the viewer feel understood before explaining anything. The programs (chapters, shipathons, free playbook) are described in plain language, almost as afterthoughts to the feeling.

### Strengths
- The opening question is a genuine hook for a talking-head video — it gives the viewer something to answer in their own head immediately.
- "Watching them get confused at the exact spot you were sure was obvious" — the details are lived-in and specific, which is what makes it sound human.
- Reframes the post-launch grind as *the fun part*, which is a more compelling sell to teenagers than "the hard part."

### Weaknesses
- Launchpad isn't named until ~40% in. On a first post from an unknown account, some viewers may not wait.
- Slightly less concrete about logistics (where, when, how to join) — it sells the feeling more than the plan.

---

## Version B — "The straight talk"

### Creative direction
Leans into the actual filming format: "this is the first video on this account, and instead of doing some big produced announcement thing, I figured I'd just look into the camera and tell you what we're building." The transparency *is* the style. It's structured like a founder update: what it is, why it needs to exist, what it looks like in practice, honest status, invitation.

### Tone and pacing
Direct and even. Talks to the viewer like a smart friend, concedes points ("And look, those are all fine"), and openly admits the current size ("we're small right now. One chapter, a handful of students") before flipping it into the pitch: "that's the best stage to join."

### Strengths
- Perfectly matched to the format — it acknowledges it's one person on camera, which makes it feel honest rather than under-produced.
- Names Launchpad in the first 20 seconds; nobody is confused about what they're watching.
- The "notice what they have in common — they all teach you how to start" argument is the clearest articulation of the actual thesis in either script.
- The smallness admission builds more trust than any amount of polish could.

### Weaknesses
- Less emotionally charged opening — it earns attention through clarity, not feeling, so it depends more on delivery.
- The middle "here's why this needs to exist" stretch is the longest continuous block; worth practicing so it doesn't flatten.

---

## Head-to-head

| Dimension | Version A | Version B |
|---|---|---|
| Opening move | Question to the viewer | Plain announcement |
| Name drop | ~40% in | ~20 seconds in |
| Emotional center | "Your project deserved users" | "Here's the gap, here's the plan" |
| Feels like | A friend who gets it | A founder leveling with you |
| Depends most on | The writing | The delivery |
| Risk | Slow reveal loses cold viewers | Flat delivery makes it a lecture |

---

## Recommendation: **Version B**

For a first video that is literally one person talking into a camera, Version B is the stronger fit:

1. **It's built for the format.** It acknowledges the setup ("just look into the camera and tell you") instead of pretending to be a produced spot. That self-awareness is exactly what made the Clicky video charming.
2. **Clarity beats suspense at zero followers.** Nobody knows Launchpad exists yet. Naming it fast and explaining it plainly respects the viewer's time; the delayed reveal in A works better once there's an audience.
3. **The honesty is the differentiator.** "We're small right now... that's the best stage to join" is the most compelling and most human moment in either script, and it's an argument only a real person on camera can make convincingly.

**Steal from A before filming:** the opening question. Starting B with "Have you ever built something you were actually proud of? Where is it now?" and *then* going into "So, this is the first video on this account..." gives you A's hook with B's clarity. Also keep A's line about watching a user "get confused at the exact spot you were sure was obvious" — drop it into B's gap paragraph if it flows when read aloud.

**Delivery note:** both scripts are written for speech, not reading. Read it out loud a few times, and wherever a sentence doesn't sound like something you'd say, change the words to yours. Drift off-script — the pauses and imperfections are the point.
