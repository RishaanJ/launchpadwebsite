# Launchpad: Vision, Mission & Goals

## Executive Summary

**Launchpad** is the high school product development network teaching students to build, ship, sell, and scale real products that reach real people—not class projects, not hackathon trophies.

---

## Core Problem

### The Gap in Education
- Most students are taught **how to code**
- Some are taught **how to pitch**
- **Very few are taught how to turn an idea into something people actually use**

### The Monday Problem
Every year, thousands of high school students build incredible projects at hackathons, coding clubs, and competitions. But come Monday:
- The project gets pushed to GitHub
- The presentation ends
- The users never come
- The product never grows

### The Real Work Begins After Launch
Learning doesn't stop once the prototype works. The hardest—and most valuable—part of building something isn't writing the first line of code. It's:
- Finding users
- Talking to customers
- Improving after launch
- Learning why people buy, share, recommend, or ignore what you create

### Geographic Inequality
- **Talent is everywhere. Opportunity isn't.**
- Some students grow up surrounded by startup founders, engineering clubs, hackathons, and mentors
- Others have none of those opportunities
- Infrastructure isn't evenly distributed—geographic location determines access to builder education

---

## The Launchpad Solution

### Mission Statement
**Build the next generation of builders by giving high school students the community, mentorship, and opportunities to create products that solve real problems.**

**We believe students are capable of far more than classroom assignments, hypothetical business plans, or one-weekend hackathon projects. They deserve the opportunity to build something that lasts.**

### Why Launchpad is Different
Most organizations focus on one piece of the puzzle. **Launchpad combines engineering, design, product thinking, marketing, and entrepreneurship into one experience—because building something people love requires all of them.**

#### Traditional Programs vs. Launchpad
| Traditional Programs | Launchpad |
|---|---|
| Learn to code | Build complete products |
| Pitch ideas | Launch real products |
| Win competitions | Grow users and impact |

| Learn one discipline | Learn the full product lifecycle |

## The Launchpad Framework: Build → Ship → Sell → Scale

### 1. **Build** (01/04)
**Identify meaningful problems and create thoughtful solutions.**

Whether software, hardware, AI, consumer products, or services, every great company begins with building. Students learn how to identify real problems and create thoughtful solutions that solve them.

### 2. **Ship** (02/04)
**Ideas don't change the world. Launched products do.**

Students learn how to publish, launch, gather feedback, and iterate quickly. Shipping is non-negotiable—ideas only matter once they're live.

### 3. **Sell** (03/04)
**A great product nobody knows about isn't enough.**

Launchpad teaches branding, storytelling, marketing, customer discovery, and distribution so products reach the people they're meant to help. Building the product is half the battle.

### 4. **Scale** (04/04)
**Launching isn't the finish line.**

Students learn how to improve products through user feedback, analytics, iteration, partnerships, and long-term thinking. Sustainable growth is the goal.

## Key Programs

### 1. **Chapters** (01/04)
Student-led communities where builders collaborate on products, host workshops, invite founders, and learn together.

**Current state:**
- 1 active chapter: AHS (Fremont)
- Target EOY 2026: 3–5 chapters

**What chapters get:**
- The full playbook + hosting kit
- Brand and identity assets
- Direct line to the founders + chapter network

**What we expect:**
- Monthly public meetings, 20+ attendees
- A selective internal operating team
- One chapter-led event in year one

### 2. **Shipathons** (02/04)
Launchpad's flagship event. Not a hackathon. Not a pitch contest. **A real product launch.**

**Format:**
- 48 hours from idea to something live
- Five tracks (Digital, Physical, Service, Social Impact, Open)
- Teams present in person to working founders, operators, and investors
- Everyone leaves with something they actually shipped—not a slide deck, not a GitHub repo. A product with users.

**Upcoming:** Spring 2027—Bay Area Shipathon (1st annual)

### 3. **Founder Sessions** (03/04)
Hear directly from founders, engineers, designers, marketers, investors, and creators about what it actually takes to build products that matter.

### 4. **Builder Resources** (04/04)
Members gain access to guides, playbooks, workshops, templates, and open-source curriculum covering every stage of product development.

## Launchpad Atlas: Global Initiative

### Vision
**Most teen builders don't have a Launchpad nearby. So we're building one that travels.**

Geography shouldn't decide whether a 16-year-old gets to start something.

### The Problem
- Talent is everywhere
- Permission to build isn't
- Chapters handle local communities, but not everyone has access to a chapter
- Infrastructure isn't evenly distributed

### What We Ship: Four Pieces
Each useful on its own:

#### 1. **Open-Source Curriculum** (01/04)
The full Launchpad playbook—meeting structures, founder-talk frameworks, build-cycle templates—published free and translated.

#### 2. **Remote Mentorship** (02/04)
Pair teen builders with operators and founders for monthly 1:1s. Async-first so timezones don't block access.

#### 3. **Regional Partners** (03/04)
Work through existing youth orgs and community spaces in target regions so the program lives where the students already are.

#### 4. **Micro-Grants** (04/04)
Small unrestricted grants ($100–$1,000) for hosting fees, hardware, and travel so cost never decides who gets to ship.

### Roadmap
- **Now:** Recruiting first regional partners + mentor cohort
- **Spring '27:** Pilot in 2 regions outside the Bay Area
- **EOY '27:** First wave of student-built products from the pilot, evaluated and shared
- **Long-term:** 3+ active global initiatives. Funded through Title-tier sponsors and grants.

### Ways to Get Involved
1. **For founders & operators:** Mentor a teen builder (1 hour/month, async-friendly)
2. **For partners:** Bring it to your region (run a youth org or community space)
3. **For funders:** Underwrite a micro-grant pool ($5K stretches across dozens of projects)
