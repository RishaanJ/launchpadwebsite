The gapWhat happens after the prototype?

Students learn to build. Few get the support to carry an idea beyond the demo.

Classes and hackathons create a powerful start. Launchpad is the continuation: time, accountability, mentors, small grants, and real users.

The usual finish line

The presentation ends.

The project reaches GitHub, a classroom, or a judging table—and often stops there.

The missing work

Users change the product.

Testing, distribution, iteration, and sustained use are where product judgment develops.

Launchpad’s role

Make shipping the start.

Teams stay together, earn resources at real milestones, and learn from what people actually do.

The cohortTeams of 2–5 · four milestone sprints

Progress unlocks resources.

Students are rewarded for evidence—not attendance. Safety, accessibility, and learning quality sit alongside product milestones.

The flagshipInaugural Bay Area Shipathon · Spring 2027

Two months to ship. One room to prove it.

Teams define a real problem, create a solution, launch it safely, and learn from real users—then present the evidence in person. Revenue is one possible path, not a requirement.

The two-month arc
WKS 1–2

Define the problem

Talk to potential users and choose a problem worth solving.

WKS 3–5

Create the solution

Build a service, app, website, physical device, or another usable product.

WKS 6–8

Launch and learn

Reach real users, iterate from evidence, and test sustainability or monetization where appropriate.

FINALE

Product fair meets pitch day

Demo and pitch to experienced judges for grand prizes and possible introductions to relevant mentors or partners.
