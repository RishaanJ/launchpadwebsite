# Launchpad Founding Sponsor Deck — Skeptic Review
*Reviewer persona: Bay Area startup CMO (real budget, can write $10K–$30K); and separately, individual founder considering a personal $500–$2,500 check.*

---

## Prioritized Fix List

### P0 — Deck-Killers (Fix Before Sending to Anyone)

1. **No team slide.** Fatal. A CMO cannot take this to a CFO, a founder cannot justify a personal check, without knowing who is running this. Zero names, zero credentials, zero advisors anywhere in 14 slides. This is the single most disqualifying omission in the deck.

2. **Traction stat is too weak and uncontextualized.** "20+ attendees at open public meetings" is the entirety of the proof-of-life (Slide 6). No cohort enrollment number, no products shipped by students, no mentors recruited, no confirmed event sponsors. "Open public meetings" sounds like a club meeting. Contextualize: how many weeks running? How many enrolled in a structured cohort? Has anyone on the cohort team shipped anything?

3. **No line-item budget.** Slide 10 lists 6 categories. Slide 13 says "$30–40K closes the entire year." That's it. A CMO's finance team will ask: what percentage goes to grants vs. operations vs. events? You have exactly this breakdown in the one-pager — it must be in the deck.

4. **Budget math inconsistency between slides.** Slide 8 (Ignition) says "$25K funds a full batch." Slide 13 says "$30–40K closes the entire year." That leaves $5–15K for the Shipathon, Atlas, chapter playbook, infrastructure, and operations combined. That can't be right, and a skeptical reader will do this math and stop trusting the numbers entirely. Fix the math or reframe the $25K claim.

---

### P1 — Significant Weaknesses (Fix Before Broad Outreach)

5. **Benefits matrix (Slide 12) is illegible.** The table text is ~20px in a 1920×1080 slide. The checkmarks render as tiny "/" characters. In any normal deck viewing context — email client, half-screen window, tablet — this is unreadable. The most important commercial slide in the deck fails at basic readability.

6. **"Six founding partner seats" definition is ambiguous.** Slide 11 says "Four tiers. Six founding partner seats." Slide 13 says "6 founding partner seats." Does this mean 6 total seats across all four tiers? Or 6 per tier? If 6 total with 4 tiers, some tiers have one seat. If it's 6 across Friend+Builder+Operator+Title, the "scarcity" claim breaks down — there can't be 1–2 Friend slots at $500 each. Clarify explicitly: "X at Title, Y at Operator, Z at Builder, open at Friend."

7. **No confirmed Shipathon details.** Slide 7 says "48 hours. Idea to live." and "Spring 2027." Has a venue been locked? Are any judges confirmed? Are the "five tracks" named anywhere? Saying "Five tracks. Judged by working founders." with zero specifics signals that none of it exists yet. Name at least one judge, one track, one venue being considered.

8. **"AHS in the Bay Area" — which school?** (Slide 6, also Slide 12 matrix). "AHS" is not a universally recognized abbreviation. Not spelling it out reads as careless and undercuts the Bay Area credibility play.

9. **The ask comes too late.** The tier pricing (Slide 11) and benefits matrix (Slide 12) are slides 11 and 12 of 14. Per the research the team commissioned, sponsors skim decks — the matrix should be visible by slide 6–7. Everything before it should earn the right to the ask, but 10 build-up slides before showing the price is too much friction.

10. **14 slides is too many for this stage.** Slides 3, 4, and 5 all explain the model/pipeline with significant overlap (Build→Ship→Sell→Scale appears in 3, the unlock table in 4 extends the same model, slide 5 re-explains the same four stages). These should collapse to 2 slides maximum.

11. **The Atlas slide (Slide 9) creates scope anxiety.** For a founding year pitch with 20 meeting attendees, introducing a "Global Initiative" pilot in "2 regions" — with no named regions, no named partners, no curriculum published — makes the org look overextended. In a first-meeting deck, Atlas is better as one bullet point on the "Where It Goes" slide, not a full slide.

12. **Cover (Slide 1) is missing standard prospectus fields.** Per MLH's guide (the research cites this directly): every prospectus cover needs projected attendance/reach, date of the flagship event, URL, and contact email visible. The cover has none of these. A CMO who opens the PDF at slide 1 and doesn't see dates or scale will assume it's aspirational vaporware.

13. **No "over-goal" unlock statement.** If someone writes a $50K check, what does that enable — a second Shipathon city, a second batch of Ignition, Atlas expansion? The research notes this and it's not in the deck. A generous potential Title sponsor has no reason to go above $30K.

14. **Individual vs. company pitch is conflated.** The callout on Slide 11 ("Individual operators and founders welcome at every tier. You don't need a logo to back a builder.") sits in a deck whose benefits matrix is entirely corporate (recruiting access, brand placement, executive advisory seat). An individual founder reading this gets mixed signals. Either build two variants or add one sentence in the matrix clarifying which rows apply to individual sponsors.

### P2 — Meaningful Improvements

15. **Hack Club Bank public ledger link should be featured upfront, not in the close.** The biggest trust objection for a new org is "where does my money actually go?" HCB's real-time public ledger answers that definitively. Mention it on Slide 10 ("Where It Goes") or add it to the cover, not just the final slide.

16. **"MONTHLY" as a standalone stat is confusing.** Slide 6 stat row: "MONTHLY / founder talks at AHS." Standing alone in a stat block, this reads as a rhythm (we meet monthly), not a number. It should read "Monthly Founder Talks" with more context, or be a specific count ("4 Talks / Since Founding").

17. **"2026" framing vs. 2027 deliverables.** The kicker on the cover says "2026." The Shipathon is Spring 2027. Ignition's first checks are Fall 2027. The founding cohort presumably runs 2026–2027. A CMO notices this and asks: "Am I sponsoring a school year that ends in 2027 or 2026 events?" Frame the founding year explicitly as "2026–27" or explain what the sponsor is funding *in 2026* vs. building toward in 2027.

18. **"In 2030, everyone will say they supported this early" (Slide 13) is too bold.** This is a great closing sentiment for an org with early traction. For a deck with 20 meeting attendees, it reads as delusional confidence. Soften: "The founding partner list closes when the founding year closes. There's no retroactive version of this."

19. **Post-event impact report promise is missing from the in-deck matrix.** The one-pager says "All tiers receive post-event impact reports." The deck's benefits matrix (Slide 12) doesn't include this row. This is the single most important stewardship signal for a first-time sponsor. It should be the top row of the matrix.

20. **Category exclusivity for Title tier is missing from the in-deck matrix.** The research document explicitly calls this out and the one-pager matrix includes it. The deck does not. Category exclusivity ("only sponsor in your space") is a real differentiator for larger company sponsors and should be in the deck.

21. **The 20.4% stat should be in the deck.** The research cites a Rice University study finding that hackathon attendees are 20.4% more likely to adopt a sponsor's platform the following year. For DevRel or developer-tools company sponsors, this is the ROI justification. It's buried in the research doc and absent from the deck.

22. **No social proof.** Not a single named mentor, advisor, or working founder who's already bought into this is cited anywhere. Even one name — "X, founder of Y, is our first mentor" — changes the credibility equation dramatically.

## Slide-by-Slide Notes

**Slide 1 — Cover**
Missing dates, projected reach, URL, and contact. The callout strip `CHAPTERS → COHORTS → SHIPATHON → IGNITION` is cryptic without context. A CMO who stops here (and many will) gets nothing.

**Slide 2 — The Problem**
This is the best slide in the deck. Crisp, specific, no empty buzzwords. The callout ("Shipping is a skill. Nobody is teaching it. We are.") lands. No changes needed.

**Slide 3 — The Model**
The four-column BUILD/SHIP/SELL/SCALE table works visually. The descriptions are concise. Minor issue: the card descriptions are about 20px in a table — borderline too small. The callout is sharp.

**Slide 4 — The Unlock System**
Good mechanics, but "Up to $1,000 grant" in the table is vague — "up to" is hedge language that undermines the unlock framing. The table mixes dollar amounts and descriptions inconsistently (Sprint 1's unlock has no dollar amount, but it's described as "resources"). Consider either quantifying all tiers or removing dollar amounts from the table and keeping them for the Ignition slide.

**Slide 5 — The Pipeline**
Redundant with slides 3 and 4. The callout ("By the time a team gets a check, they've already shipped") is the best sentence on this slide and should be promoted to headline or saved for slide 8. Consider cutting this slide entirely and folding its visual into slide 3.

**Slide 6 — Year One**
The stat row is the problem slide. "20+" is brutally thin. "MONTHLY" reads as a cadence, not a count. "SPRING 2027" isn't a Year One stat, it's a Year Two deliverable. The body copy pre-empts the objection well ("We won't pretend this is traction at scale") but the stat row contradicts the honesty — those three numbers don't actually make the case. What to replace with: total cohort enrollees, total mentor hours committed, total number of working founders who've done a talk (even if 1–2), anything that's a discrete achievement rather than an operating rhythm.

**Slide 7 — The Shipathon**
"Five tracks. Judged by working founders." These tracks need names. Are they tech, social, hardware, etc.? What does "working founder" mean — YC-backed, indie? Zero specifics. The 48-hour format is compelling and differentiated, but the slide delivers almost no evidence that this event is going to happen. No venue, no named judges, no confirmed sponsors. At minimum: "Planning partners include [X venue] and [Y founder]."

**Slide 8 — Launchpad Ignition**
"Written at admission, not at demo day" is genuinely distinctive and the slide buries it in body copy. Make that the callout or the headline. The "$25K funds a full batch" stat needs to reconcile with the overall $30–40K ask (see P0 #4).

The phrase "Teams keep 100%: checks are grants through our 501(c)(3), no equity, no cap table before you can vote" is legally dense for a sponsorship deck. Simplify: "Grants, not investments. No equity, no strings." Put the legal mechanics in the FAQ.

**Slide 9 — Launchpad Atlas**
The headline ("Talent is everywhere. Permission to build is not.") is the second-best headline in the deck — and it's wasted on a slide that underdelivers on it. "2 regions, pilot, Spring 2027" with no named regions, no named partners, no curriculum URL. Either substantiate this or cut the slide.

**Slide 10 — What Your Money Does**
Good concept, but "Software & infrastructure" and "Operations" are generic budget lines every nonprofit has. Make them specific: "Infrastructure: $2K — Notion, Vercel, domain, communication tools for 6 chapters." The $30–40K goal is mentioned but with no allocation percentages. A CMO's question is: "Is most of this going to events, or is it mostly going to two founders' stipends?" Answer that question here, not in a follow-up.

**Slide 11 — The Ask**
Visually this is the best "tier card" execution in the deck. The OPEN/FEATURED labels work. The four cards are scannable. Two issues: "Six founding partner seats" is undefined (see P1 #6), and Friend at $500 has the copy "Back the mission" which is fine, but Builder's "Mentor the next generation" implies the $2,500 includes time commitment — is that clearly opt-in?

**Slide 12 — Tier Benefits**
Biggest execution failure. The font is too small, the "/" checkmarks are invisible at normal viewing sizes, and the matrix in the deck is missing rows that appear in the one-pager (category exclusivity, post-event report). Also: "Quarterly impact email from founding team" appears for Friend and Builder but NOT for Operator and Title — is that a typo? Surely top-tier sponsors get at least that plus more, not minus it. The matrix as written implies Operator and Title get less proactive communication in some rows than Friend/Builder.

Wait — re-reading the copy: the matrix shows quarterly impact emails for Friend and Builder, "Quarterly progress reports" for Operator, and "Executive advisory seat" for Title. The labeling inconsistency (email vs. reports vs. seat) implies it's a different type, but visually it looks like Operator/Title don't get the email. Needs clearer visual treatment.

**Slide 13 — Founding Partner Seats**
The "In 2030" callout is too aggressive for current traction level (see P2 #18). The stat row is clean and works. The body copy is the strongest close argument in the deck. The "founding partner is a title nobody can buy later" line is genuinely good.

**Slide 14 — Transparency & Close**
The email address (`team@buildlaunchpad.org`) should appear earlier in the deck and should be on every slide footer, not just the final CTA. "One conversation closes this" is a solid closer but it's preceded by a paragraph of logistics. Consider: lead with the CTA ("Claim a founding seat. One conversation.") and put the fine print below. Currently the fine print buries the ask.

The 7-day seat hold should be stated earlier (e.g., slide 13), not only in the close. It creates urgency but the reader only sees it at the end of a 14-slide deck.

## Deck-Wide Observations

**What makes me say yes (as a CMO):**
- HCB fiscal sponsorship with public ledger is a genuine structural differentiator — most first-year student orgs have none of this
- The model is intellectually coherent and differentiated from hackathons
- The "milestones unlock resources" mechanic is easy to explain to a CFO
- The copy is sharper than 95% of what I see from student organizations
- $10K Operator is priced right for a DevRel or early-stage company budget

**What makes me say no (as a CMO):**
- I cannot bring a deck with no team slide to my leadership team
- "20 people at meetings" is not enough to justify $10K+ to a CFO
- No venue, no confirmed judges, no confirmed anything for the Shipathon
- The budget math doesn't add up under scrutiny

**What makes me write a personal $500–$2,500 check (as a founder):**
- The "I wish this existed when I was 16" hook is real — the copy earns it
- $500 is low-stakes enough to try before the evidence is in
- The transparency infrastructure (HCB) means I can watch where my money goes
- The copy is honest about being early ("conviction and a plan you can audit")

**What stops my personal check:**
- Still no names — I'd want to know the founder is a real person I could verify
- I'd want a LinkedIn before wiring money, even to an HCB account

**The deck's structural original sin:** It's built for an asynchronous cold read but it's trying to do meeting-deck work. A 14-slide deck with dense body copy and an illegible matrix is neither a cold-read one-pager nor a meeting deck — it's in between and serves neither purpose well. The fix: build two artifacts. (1) An 8-slide meeting deck (cover, problem, model, traction, ask, matrix, transparency, close) with large type and no body text. (2) The one-pager (already exists) for email. The current deck is too long for email and too text-heavy for a room.
