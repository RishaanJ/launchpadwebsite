# Sponsorship Deck — Research Report

Research across four areas: comparable-org prospectuses (Hack Club, MLH, FIRST, DECA, high-school hackathons), sponsor motivations, first-timer objections, and deck-structure best practices.

## (a) Concrete recommendations for the deck

**1. Keep it to 8–10 slides (or a 2–3 page PDF), and never send it in the first email.**
MLH's organizer guide is blunt: sponsors don't read long decks; it recommends 2–3 pages, minimal text, and warns that the #1 mistake is "overly complex, lengthy sponsorship decks." It also has a hard rule: the first outreach email should contain *no links, attachments, or images* (spam filters + skimming execs) — send the prospectus in the follow-up. Build a 10-slide deck for meetings and a 2-page PDF "one-pager+matrix" for email.

**2. Order slides as a journey from "you" to "them" — and get to the matrix fast.**
Consensus structure: (1) cover with event/org name, dates, projected reach, contact; (2) what Launchpad is, in one slide; (3) who you reach (audience slide: # chapters, # students, Bay Area schools, demographics); (4) why sponsors win (mapped to their motivations, not your mission); (5) the benefits matrix; (6) "Where your money goes" budget slide; (7) team + fiscal sponsorship trust slide; (8) roadmap (Shipathon Spring 2027, Ignition); (9) the ask + contact. MLH says every prospectus must have the basics visible: name, date, projected attendance, URL, email.

**3. Run two versions of the "why sponsor" slide — one for companies, one for individual founders.**
MLH identifies three corporate motivations: product feedback/adoption (DevRel budgets), recruitment, and brand awareness — tailor per target. Rice Business research found developers who attended a sponsored hackathon were **20.4% more likely to adopt the sponsor's platform the next year** — use that for company pitches. Friend/Builder tiers will mostly come from individual founders/operators; Hack Club's philanthropy page sells legacy and scale and runs a public named donor wall. Founders give for goodwill and "I wish this existed when I was 16" — sell that, not logo placement.

**4. Add a "Where will your money go?" slide with line items tied to tiers.**
The single most consistent trust-builder across comparables. LingHacks (Bay Area HS hackathon, same fiscal-sponsorship structure) devotes a full page to it. Make tiers concrete: "$500 = 5 micro-grant unlocks," "$2,500 = ships hardware to 3 new chapters," "$10,000 = funds one Shipathon site end-to-end."

**5. Weaponize your fiscal sponsorship — HCB is a transparency feature, not fine print.**
Hack Club Bank means the ledger can be *public*, 990s auto-filed, umbrella org (The Hack Foundation, 501(c)(3), externally audited since 2021) known to every Bay Area tech donor. Put the HCB public-ledger link directly on the trust slide. No established nonprofit can beat a brand-new org on transparency — this flips your biggest weakness.

**6. Sell employee engagement and brand affinity at the top tiers — not resume books.**
The audience is minors, so college-hackathon recruiting perks don't translate. FIRST's substitute: employee engagement through mentorship/judging, sponsor reports with demographics and outcomes, brand recognition with future engineers. Frame Operator/Title as "your engineers mentor and judge; your tools are what 500 future founders learn on; you meet your 2031 intern class first."

**7. Engineer scarcity and a small wedge.**
LingHacks capped its top tier at two partners; make "6 Founding Partner seats, never available again" a literal design element (six boxes, some filled). $500 Friend is the objection-killer wedge — a low-commitment entry lets a skeptical first-time sponsor "try" you. State that all tiers are negotiable and custom packages welcome.

**8. Promise the post-event impact report in the deck itself.**
DECA and FIRST emphasize stewardship: results reports, demographics, photos, thank-yous. Add to every tier: "All sponsors receive a post-Shipathon impact report (projects shipped, students reached, media, photos)." For first-time sponsors of a new org, a promised report is the renewal mechanism.

## (c) Top 5 objections and the one-line answer to bake in

1. **"You have no track record."** → "Our founding team has [shipped X / run Y], we operate inside Hack Club's audited 501(c)(3) infrastructure, and here is the week-by-week Year 1 plan and budget."
2. **"Where does my money actually go?"** → "Our books are public in real time on Hack Club Bank — here's the line-item budget and exactly what your tier funds."
3. **"Is this legit / is it tax-deductible?"** → "Launchpad is fiscally sponsored by The Hack Foundation (501(c)(3), EIN 81-2908499, externally audited); sponsorships are tax-deductible as charitable gifts or deductible as ordinary marketing expense."
4. **"What's the ROI — they're high schoolers, not hires?"** → "Developers are 20.4% more likely to adopt a sponsor's platform after building on it (Rice research) — these are the Bay Area's next founders and interns, meeting your brand first."
5. **"What if it flops / never happens?"** → "Funds sit with our fiscal sponsor until milestones hit, the Shipathon has a locked date and venue plan, and at $500–$2,500 you can start small — we'll earn the renewal with the impact report."
